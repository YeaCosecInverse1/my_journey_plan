# EUROPEAN UNIVERSITY STRATEGY

## Goal

Get a legitimate, relevant Bachelor's in Europe that supports:
1. a strong CS/engineering foundation,
2. affordable study,
3. internships and employment,
4. future Japan HSP strategy,
5. realistic admission.

## Preferred degree families

Tier 1:
- Computer Science
- Computer Engineering
- Information Engineering

Tier 2:
- Software Engineering
- Information Technology
- closely related technical degrees

Curriculum matters more than the title.

## University decision matrix

Score each candidate on:

1. HSP qualifying-university eligibility.
2. Degree relevance.
3. English-taught availability.
4. Total yearly cost.
5. Scholarship/fee-reduction possibilities.
6. Admission probability.
7. Internship/job ecosystem.
8. Normal graduation duration.
9. International-student support.
10. Technical curriculum quality.

## HSP university bonus rule

A qualifying university can be strategically valuable because the HSP system has a university-related bonus. Do not assume that multiple university routes stack into multiple identical bonuses. Verify the current official category and the specific university at the time of application.

## Research direction from the archive

Italy emerged as a particularly strong financial/strategic fit, with University of Padua and Sapienza University of Rome repeatedly relevant to the earlier research. Finland can be academically attractive but may require a stronger financial plan. The Netherlands tends to be expensive under the user's budget assumptions.

These are candidates, not permanent commitments. Re-check tuition, admissions, scholarships, programme language, housing and HSP eligibility before applying.

## What makes a university bad for this plan

- weak technical curriculum
- financially unsustainable cost
- unclear degree recognition
- poor internship ecosystem
- overly long route with little value
- selecting only because of a single HSP bonus

## Application strategy

Never apply to one university only.

Use three layers:
- ambitious
- realistic
- safety

Keep the degree/career fit high across all three.
