# 1. আগে সবচেয়ে গুরুত্বপূর্ণ distinction

## JLPT ≠ Japanese mastery

JLPT-এর official test structure-এ vocabulary/grammar, reading এবং listening assess করা হয়; speaking এবং writing আলাদা করে পরীক্ষা করা হয় না। N3, N4, N5-এর sections এবং সময় official site-এ নির্দিষ্ট।

তাই তিনটি scoreboard রাখবে:

| Track | কী মাপে | Target |
|---|---|---|
| **JLPT** | Exam performance | N5 → N4 → N3, high practice accuracy |
| **Language ability** | Reading, listening, speaking, writing | বাস্তব Japanese ব্যবহার |
| **Native ability** | Japanese media + Japanese-only processing | Translation dependency কমানো |

### তোমার 90–95% goal কীভাবে বুঝবে

Actual JLPT-কে “95% marks” হিসেবে ভাবা ঠিক নয়। JLPT results scaled scoring ব্যবহার করে এবং section-level minimum-ও থাকে। তাই personal target হবে:

> **Official-format timed practice-এ ধারাবাহিকভাবে ~90–95% raw accuracy**, সব section-এ strong performance সহ।

এটা **practice benchmark**, official JLPT percentage নয়।

# 4. Phase 0 — Absolute Zero

## 4.1 Japanese আসলে কী?

Japanese writing system-এ মূলত তিনটি script system একসাথে কাজ করে:

- **ひらがな — Hiragana**: grammatical endings, native words, many beginner texts
- **カタカナ — Katakana**: loanwords, foreign names, emphasis, onomatopoeia ইত্যাদি
- **漢字 — Kanji**: lexical meaning-bearing characters

এছাড়া Latin alphabet, Arabic numerals, punctuation ও symbols-ও দেখা যায়।

### প্রথম ভুলটি করো না

Romanized Japanese দিয়ে মাসের পর মাস পড়া যাবে না।

```text
あさ
```

শেষ লক্ষ্য:

```text
あさ → Japanese reading
```

না যে:

```text
あさ → a-sa → meaning
```

## Preview

- lesson goal দেখো
- dialogue শুনো
- নতুন vocabulary identify করো
- grammar pattern mark করো

## Understand

প্রথমবার সব grammar analyse করার দরকার নেই। Situation বুঝো।

## Vocabulary

Important শব্দ Anki-তে যাবে। সব শব্দ নয়।

## Grammar

## Shadow

Audio-এর সাথে timing, rhythm, pauses, intonation match করো।

## Produce

নিজের 3–5টি sentence তৈরি করো।

## Recall

Book বন্ধ করে:

- grammar explain করতে পারি?
- 2–3টি নিজের sentence বানাতে পারি?
- dialogue-এর meaning ধরতে পারি?
- key vocabulary recall করতে পারি?

# 12. Vocabulary + SRS

## Anki = memory engine

Anki Japanese course নয়।

Anki-এর কাজ:

> এমন vocabulary/grammar যেন বারবার forgetting-এর পরেও long-term memory-তে থাকে।

### Daily order

```text
Reviews first
   ↓
New cards
   ↓
Native/context exposure
```

Review backlog জমিয়ে ফেলো না।

## Card philosophy

Beginner stage-এ bilingual support okay। কিন্তু ধীরে ধীরে context-based cards বাড়াও।

Better:

```text
昨日、ラーメンを食べました。
```

than only:

```text
食べる = to eat
```

কারণ sentence-এ vocabulary + grammar + context + reading একসাথে আসে।

## Important correction

“Sentence card = scientifically always superior” — এমন absolute claim করা ঠিক নয়। Card design learner, level, target এবং recall type অনুযায়ী বদলাবে।

> **যখন Japanese explanation বুঝতে পারো, তখন English explanation-কে first resort বানিও না।**

Unknown abstract vocabulary, technical term বা subtle distinction-এ bilingual fallback রাখা completely fine.

# 24. Comprehensible Input

# 28. Register / 文体 — Advanced writing-এর hidden difficulty

Japanese writing-এ শুধু grammar correct হলেই যথেষ্ট নয়। পুরো paragraph-এর register consistent হওয়া দরকার।

উদাহরণস্বরূপ:

```text
formal です・ます
↓
হঠাৎ casual だった
↓
আবার academic である
```

প্রতিটি sentence আলাদা করে ঠিক হলেও পুরো document awkward হতে পারে।

তাই advanced writing-এ **document-level correction** গুরুত্বপূর্ণ।

# 29. Sentence Mining

Native content থেকে useful sentence সংগ্রহ করা powerful হতে পারে।

Ideal mined sentence:

- context meaningful
- বেশিরভাগ অংশ known
- একটি নতুন vocabulary/grammar point আছে
- natural Japanese
- audio/context পাওয়া যায়

### N+1 rule

“N+1” একটি useful heuristic; universal law নয়।

Sentence-এ যদি 5টি unknown থাকে, সেটা SRS card হিসেবে অতিরিক্ত difficult হতে পারে। এমন sentence নাও যেটা context-এর সাহায্যে reasonably recover করা যায়।

### Card-এর ideal source

```text
নিজে যে Japanese পড়েছি/শুনেছি
        ↓
interesting sentence
        ↓
Yomitan lookup
        ↓
meaning + reading + audio
        ↓
Anki
```

এতে deck তোমার actual Japanese exposure-এর mirror হয়।

# 36. JLPT Scoring — ভুল ধারণা বাদ

≠
necessarily 60/100 final scaled score

# 81. Reading as a Japanese Person Would

At high level, reading becomes less about translation.

You move from:

```text
word → English → sentence
```

to:

```text
sentence → situation
```

then:

```text
paragraph → intent
```

then:

```text
text → implication
```

That is where real reading proficiency lives.

---

# 82. Listening as a Japanese Person Would

Eventually you should not hear:

```text
音 → word1 → word2 → word3 → translation
```

but approximately:

```text
sound stream → segmented meaning → situation
```

That requires repeated exposure.

The goal is not to listen to 100 “perfectly studied” clips.

The goal is to eventually process **thousands of minutes of understandable Japanese**.

---

# 83. Speaking as a Japanese Learner vs Speaker

At beginner level:

> “I know the grammar but I cannot produce it.”

Normal.

The cure is:

```text
recall
+
sentence generation
+
conversation
+
correction
+
repetition
```

Do not wait until N3 to speak.

You can begin very simply:

```text
これは何ですか。
これは本です。
どこですか。
駅です。
```

Then scale.

# 84. The Native-Input Threshold

You do not need to “finish N3” before touching native Japanese.

Start easy native-ish material early.

But the amount changes:

```text
N5 → tiny doses
N4 → regular exposure
N3 → serious native input
N2 → immersion dominates
N1+ → Japanese becomes the environment
```

This distinction preserves the source files’ strongest idea while avoiding the false claim that beginners should immediately consume raw native media for hours.

# 87. Cultural / Pragmatic Competence

Grammar alone cannot tell you:

- how direct to be;
- when an apology is expected;
- how much softening is natural;
- how a request changes by relationship;
- how silence functions;
- how indirectness communicates disagreement;
- what degree of politeness is appropriate.

So later study should include **pragmatics**.

This is also why human interaction becomes more important at N3+.

# 88. A Note on “Thinking in Japanese”

Do not try to force it.

Instead reduce translation dependency naturally:

```text

```

One day you notice you understood something without translating it.

That is the real milestone.

# 93. Resource Failure Diagnosis

When progress stalls, do not immediately find a new app.

Diagnose the bottleneck.

## “I know grammar but cannot understand speech.”

Increase:

- listening volume;
- easy audio;
- shadowing;
- sentence segmentation practice.

## “I know words but cannot read.”

Increase:

- reading volume;
- kanji-in-context exposure;
- graded reading.

## “I understand everything but cannot speak.”

Increase:

- retrieval;
- sentence generation;
- conversation;
- recording;
- correction.

## “I can pass practice tests but media destroys me.”

Increase:

- native input;
- casual speech;
- contractions;
- speed;
- subtitle reduction.

## “Anki is taking forever.”

Reduce:

- new cards;
- low-value cards;
- duplicate cards.

Then review FSRS settings and card quality.

# 101. What Was Deliberately Removed or Corrected From the Source Drafts

The supplied material was rich, but an “ultimate guide” should also know what **not** to publish as fact.

### Removed / softened

- “Romaji irreparably damages the brain.”
- “Every valid Japanese sentence contains an invisible が.”
- “One exact theory of は/が is the only structural truth.”
- Highly specific neuroscience claims about pitch accent processing and permanent foreign-accent formation.
- Universal claims that FSRS saves a fixed 20–30% of reviews for everyone.
- Exact Tadoku word-count equivalences presented as if official JLPT standards.
- “N+1 must mean exactly one unknown item.”
- Hard claims that N2 is universally required for office work.
- Overly specific interpretations of JLPT scaling/IRT not established by the official pages reviewed.
- Assuming every free platform feature remains free indefinitely.
- Treating Marugoto’s entire ecosystem as a permanently free full textbook replacement.

### Retained

- parallel structure + immersion;
- Irodori as the practical spine;
- grammar as a conceptual system;
- Anki/FSRS as the memory engine;
- graded reading;
- early listening;
- shadowing;
- deliberate output;
- “tomorrow’s lesson today”;
- official JLPT as authority;
- error logs;
- progressively less English;
- Yomitan/jpdb for native-content learning;
- native media as the long-term environment.

This is the difference between **compiling notes** and building a reliable guide.
