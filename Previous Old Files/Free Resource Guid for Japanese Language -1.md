# 🥇 PART I: ABSOLUTE ZERO

## Stage 0: Understand what Japanese actually is

Before memorizing hundreds of words, understand the writing system.

Japanese uses:

```text
HIRAGANA
↓
ひらがな

KATAKANA
↓
カタカナ

KANJI
↓
漢字
```

Plus:

```text
Latin alphabet
numbers
punctuation
symbols
```

Hiragana and katakana represent the same basic sound system, while kanji carries lexical meaning. JapanesePod101's free kana material provides charts, stroke-order information, worksheets and quizzes.

### 🎯 Goal

Do **not** spend months “studying kana.”

You need:

- Hiragana
- Katakana
- dakuten
- handakuten
- small ゃゅょ
- small っ
- long vowels
- contracted sounds
- basic mora timing

---

### Primary

### Procedure


But here's the important part:

### ❌ Do NOT learn:

> あ = a  
> い = i  
> う = u

forever.

That creates dependency on romaji.

Instead:

> あ → **あ**

Your brain should eventually see:

```text
あさ
```

and immediately process it as Japanese.

Not:

```text
a-sa
```

# 🔊 Stage 2: Japanese pronunciation

This should happen **very early**, not at N2.

Learn:

- mora timing
- vowel length
- ん
- っ
- devoiced vowels
- ふ
- ら-row
- ザ/ダ distinctions
- や/ゆ/よ
- pitch accent
- sentence intonation

### Free resources

Kanshudo explains the four major pitch patterns and provides native audio examples.

Also use:

Do **not** obsess over pitch accent for every word on Day 1.

Instead:

```text
Stage 0–1
↓
correct sounds + mora timing

Stage N5
↓
understand pitch exists

Stage N4
↓
begin actively noticing it

Stage N3+
↓
actively reproduce it
```

Japanese is pitch-accented, not stress-accented in the English sense. Kanshudo's explanation is particularly useful here.

# How to use Tae Kim

**Do not treat it as your main textbook.**

Use:

```text

      ↓

      ↓
"I don't fully understand this."
      ↓

      ↓

      ↓

      ↓

```


> たい = want to

```text

↓

```

That difference is enormous.

# 🧠 Grammar mastery rule

For every important grammar structure:

### Level 1

Recognize it.

### Level 2

Understand it.

### Level 3

### Level 4

### Level 5

Understand it instantly in native speech.

### Level 6

Use it naturally without mentally translating.

**Level 6 is the real target.**


# ⚠️ One important correction to your “90–95% N5/N4/N3” goal

Your goal is good, but don't confuse **exam score with mastery**.

A 95% N3 score does **not** mean:

> “I understand 95% of Japanese.”

Likewise, someone can pass N3 and still struggle badly with anime, manga, casual speech, workplace conversation, or spontaneous speaking.

The JLPT itself does not test speaking or writing.

```text
                 JAPANESE ABILITY
```
